About Denim

From Hollywood sets
to African boardrooms.

Denim Richards is a Paramount series regular on Yellowstone — one of the most strategically ambitious creative minds to emerge from Hollywood in a generation. He is a producer, director, and creative economy architect, and the CEO of Opulent Entertainment Group.

As Global Director for Media, Arts and Culture under the African Union Sixth Region mandate, Denim operates at the highest level of creative economy policy — advising governments, cultural institutions, and sovereign agencies across the continent. His mandate carries a presidential endorsement from H.E. Mokgweetsi Masisi, Former President of the Republic of Botswana, placing him at the intersection of diplomatic access and production infrastructure.

His production footprint spans 7 countries through Opulent Entertainment Group, backed by a co-production partnership with Primedia — Africa's largest media group — delivering African versions of The Voice, Idols, Project Runway, and Dancing with the Stars to audiences across the continent. His University of Botswana masterclass drew 1,300+ attendees. He has keynoted the UNWTO Africa Youth in Tourism Innovation Summit in Namibia and delivered panels from Botswana to South Africa alongside Billboard Africa and Primedia leadership.

His methodology is direct: structure first, content follows. Every engagement — artist, institution, or sovereign — starts by installing the commercial backbone. IP frameworks. Licensing architecture. Distribution pipelines. Then content is created to scale.

The Work

Africa is not an emerging opportunity.
It is the next entertainment economy.

The work Denim does — at the artist level, the institutional level, and the sovereign level — is to install the commercial backbone that allows creative talent to scale.

The Thesis

Structure precedes content.

01

Structure First. Content Follows.

Licensing frameworks, royalty systems, and distribution pipelines must come before the campaigns. Without commercial backbone, even brilliant content can't scale.

02

Talent + Ownership.

Artists scale when they own their IP, their data, and their distribution. Mentorship has to teach the business, not just the craft.

03

Cultural Authority = Soft Power.

Stories build markets. Markets build economies. Entertainment is one of Africa's most underdeployed strategic assets.

04

Hollywood Standards. African Specificity.

Bring the rigor of a Paramount production. Apply the texture of an African market. Refuse the false choice between excellence and authenticity.

Why Now

The structural window is closing.

Africa's creative economy is worth an estimated $31–59 billion today. BCG projects $140–150 billion by 2030. Brookings projects $200 billion. Governments in Rwanda, Nigeria, Ghana, and South Africa are actively allocating budget to creative economy infrastructure — right now.

The institutions that install the structure first will own the market. The artists who build ownership into their careers now will not be replaced by the next generation — they will become the infrastructure the next generation runs on. The window for first-mover positioning is open. It will not stay open.
